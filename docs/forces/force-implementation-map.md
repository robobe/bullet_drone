## Owning stage and verification

Control produces desired thrust/torque; the mixer allocates rotor targets. Neither stage applies physical forces. Actual rotor speeds feed the baseline propeller model and contextual ForceChain; the PyBullet adapter validates and applies their final outputs before one integration step. Gravity and collision response are solver-owned.

## Frames, ownership and required boundaries

- World Z points up. Body X is forward, Y left, Z up. `DroneState` linear and angular velocities are **world-frame**; [IdealStateFeedback](../../src/bullet_drone/controllers/internal.py) converts angular velocity to body rates. Aerodynamic models must make this conversion explicit. Per-rotor forces and aggregate reaction torque in [PropellerOutput](../../src/bullet_drone/models.py) are body-frame N and N·m, applied with `LINK_FRAME`.
- Rotor speeds are **rad/s**, not RPM. [Vehicle parameter loading](../../src/bullet_drone/vehicle.py) converts RPM-squared thrust/torque coefficients by multiplying by `(60 / (2π))²`. A rotor-drag coefficient fitted to RPM requires the corresponding *linear* speed conversion; do not reuse it unchanged with rad/s.
- The mixer predicts a wrench using static ideal actuator coefficients. Its `achieved` wrench is an allocation estimate, not measured post-lag/post-aerodynamics thrust. Models of wind, drag, voltage or ground effect belong downstream; allocation-aware capacity changes can be fed back deliberately in a later design.
- Apply gravity and contact only through PyBullet. Apply each rotor force once at its rotor position; PyBullet then supplies its lever-arm torque. Do not also apply the mixer's achieved roll/pitch torque as an external torque. Apply reaction yaw torque separately once.
- Keep each new effect in a named method or focused module, with pure calculation separated from PyBullet application. Existing motor injection is sufficient for lag. State-dependent aerodynamics are composed downstream of the unchanged speed-only propeller contract by ForceChain, with pre-step state, actual rotor speeds and project-owned read-only surface queries. Rendering must not become the source of physical surface queries.
- Mass, inertia, rotor geometry, collision shapes and timestep are model/integration properties, not additional force rows. They are configured by [simulation configuration](../../src/bullet_drone/config.py), vehicle parameters and URDF; PyBullet handles rigid-body integration.

## Gyroscopic sign and course-model caveats

With physical rotor spin signs `s_i` around body +Z, define rotor momentum as `H = Σ(s_i J_i ω_i) z_body`. For prescribed steady rotor speed, the reaction on the body from transporting that momentum is `−Ω_body × H`. The positive `Ω × H` is the torque required **on the rotor**. Changing which body owns a torque changes the sign.

The [course implementation](https://github.com/amire2000/drone-physics-and-simulation-course/blob/main/examples/common/drone_physics.py#L183-L194) uses its yaw-reaction signs to construct a signed quantity and applies the positive cross product. That signed quantity is not automatically physical rotor momentum: aerodynamic body-reaction signs are opposite physical spin signs. Define and test the convention before porting this formula. Rotor acceleration reaction and rigid-body Euler/inertia dynamics are separate concerns; PyBullet already integrates the rigid body, so do not add its inertia coupling again.

The same [course source](https://github.com/amire2000/drone-physics-and-simulation-course/blob/main/examples/common/drone_physics.py#L109-L182) uses simplified motor lag and bounded empirical inflow/ground-effect/flapping models. The implemented local variants are configurable teaching approximations, not calibrated models for this vehicle. See [aerodynamic equations and defaults](../design/aerodynamics.md).
